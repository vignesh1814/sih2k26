SIH26034 Research & Pre-Engineering Dossier
A. Executive Summary
This dossier delivers a comprehensive pre-engineering analysis and architectural blueprint for Smart India Hackathon (SIH) 2026 Problem Statement SIH26034. The fundamental challenge involves designing a software system capable of verifying the compliance of packaged commodities against the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, leveraging optical scans of products, images, and labels1 [FACT]. The underlying engineering complexity emerges at the intersection of computer vision (CV) for spatial localization, optical character recognition (OCR) for transcription, structured information extraction, and deterministic rules-based validation.
The analysis concludes that while extracting textual declarations and performing deterministic validations are highly automatable using modern multimodal pipelines—specifically pairing high-accuracy OCR engines like PaddleOCR with strict schema-enforced Large Language Models (LLMs)—the stringent geometric and physical measurement requirements of the legal framework present profound challenges [ENGINEERING-INFERENCE]. Specifically, the mandates governing the absolute physical height of numerals in millimeters and the precise area of the Principal Display Panel (PDP) cannot be reliably resolved via uncalibrated monocular vision systems without physical reference objects [FACT]3. Consequently, the recommended system architecture aggressively optimizes for a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) paradigm. High-confidence deterministic checks are fully automated, while spatial compliances and anomalous detections trigger probabilistic warnings requiring human validation, ensuring the system remains legally defensible.
B. SIH26034 Problem Analysis
The official problem statement mandates the creation of a "Software System to check compliance of Packaged Commodities under Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 by scanning products, images and labels"1 [FACT]. The overarching mission is discovery and regulatory enforcement automation.
By deconstructing the problem statement, the core operations require the system to ingest varied images of commodity packaging, encompassing diverse topographies such as flat cartons, cylindrical bottles, and reflective flexible pouches. Once ingested, the system must localize and extract relevant text, spatial features, and barcodes. These extracted entities must then be semantically mapped to the mandatory declarations defined by the Legal Metrology Rules [SOURCED-INTERPRETATION]. Crucially, the system must execute a temporally versioned rule engine to verify the presence, format, and mathematical validity of these declarations against the specific rule set active at the time of the product's manufacture, ultimately flagging violations and generating an auditable compliance report [ENGINEERING-INFERENCE].

D. Legal / Regulatory Framework
The compliance framework is rigidly anchored by the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, and the subsequent Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 20118 [FACT]. These rules establish strict guidelines dictating the information that must be presented to consumers, the specific typography formatting, and the geometric placement of these declarations.
A 'pre-packaged commodity' is defined as a commodity placed in a package of whatever nature, without the purchaser being present, such that the product has a pre-determined quantity9 [FACT]. The primary legislative vehicle is Rule 6, which outlines the mandatory declarations required on all standard packages. These include the full name and address of the manufacturer, packer, or importer; the country of origin for imported goods; the generic name of the commodity; the net quantity in standard units; the month and year of manufacture; the Maximum Retail Price (MRP) inclusive of all taxes; comprehensive consumer care details; and, crucially following recent amendments, the unit sale price9 [FACT].
Rule 7 governs the Principal Display Panel (PDP), mandating that all essential information must appear grouped on this panel to ensure consumer visibility11. The PDP area is mathematically defined based on the shape of the packaging—for instance, 40% of the surface area for cylindrical packages12 [FACT]. Further complexity is introduced by Tables I and II associated with Rule 7, which specify the absolute minimum height in millimeters for numerals and letters, scaled directly to either the net quantity or the PDP area5. Finally, Rule 26 provides critical exemptions, specifically noting that packages containing 10 grams or 10 milliliters or less are exempt from several labeling mandates15 [FACT]. The strict adherence to standard units is also heavily monitored; utilizing non-standard abbreviations such as "gms" instead of "g", or "ltrs" instead of "L", constitutes a direct technical violation18 [SOURCED-INTERPRETATION].

G. Literature Survey (Overview)
The challenge of automating compliance for visually rich documents (VRDs) and complex product packaging relies on the convergence of Scene Text Recognition, Object Detection, and Key Information Extraction (KIE). Historically, engineering pipelines utilized a highly sequential approach: image binarization followed by OCR, culminating in Regular Expressions to find dates and numbers. While deterministic, this approach catastrophically fails on crumpled packaging, variable lighting, and arbitrary label designs common in Indian FMCG sectors [FACT].
Recent literature highlights a decisive shift toward multimodal document understanding. Transformer-based architectures, notably the LayoutLM family, natively encode textual, spatial (bounding box coordinates), and visual (image patch) features to understand the semantic structure of a document27 [SOURCED-INTERPRETATION]. Concurrently, large vision-language models (VLMs) such as Qwen2-VL and Florence-2 have demonstrated robust zero-shot capabilities, extracting structured JSON schemas directly from complex visual scenes without an explicit OCR intermediate step28 [FACT].

N. Readability / Font / Placement Research
The central technical limitation of the SIH26034 mandate revolves around Rule 7 and Tables I and II, which dictate that the physical height of numerals in the net quantity declaration must meet absolute millimeter thresholds based on the package size5 [FACT].
The critical engineering question is whether legal font-size compliance can be reliably established from an arbitrary photograph. The conclusion is strictly no. Monocular vision systems, such as standard smartphone cameras, capture light in a pixel matrix, possessing no absolute scale. Without active depth sensing hardware (like Time-of-Flight or LiDAR sensors) or a known physical reference object in the frame, calculating a pixel-to-millimeter ratio is mathematically unsolvable due to unknown perspective projection and focal distance variables [FACT]3.
Methods such as attempting to infer the dimensions of the package using a product database fail if the product is a new variant or a counterfeit. Consequently, true physical measurement via image processing must be classified as an UNRESOLVED constraint. For the SIH MVP, the system must utilize an AR-assisted UI to enforce a physical reference object protocol (e.g., placing an ID card next to the label), or alternatively, explicitly flag font-size compliance as "Requires Human Verification" [RECOMMENDATION].
O. Product Classification Research
LMPC compliance rules vary significantly by product category. Determining the broad product class is the mandatory first step in routing extracted data to the correct rule set. Research into existing taxonomies highlights datasets like SKU-110K, which provides 11,743 densely packed retail shelf images suitable for localization training55, and the RPC (Retail Product Checkout) dataset, which contributes 83,739 standardized images across 200 distinct SKUs57 [FACT].
The recommended approach utilizes a hierarchical classification model fine-tuned on RPC and localized Indian grocery datasets59 to classify the commodity type. This classification subsequently queries a metadata database to retrieve specific LMPC exception rules applicable exclusively to that category [ENGINEERING-INFERENCE].
P. Multilingual Research
The real-world packaging landscape in India is defined by deep linguistic diversity, frequently employing bilingual (English and Hindi), code-switched, or transliterated typography. The Bharat Scene Text Dataset (BSTD) serves as the primary benchmark, highlighting the severe complexities of Indic script OCR in the wild across 6,582 scene images7 [FACT]. Standard models trained exclusively on Latin characters predictably fail to extract generic names or manufacturer addresses printed in regional languages.
PaddleOCR provides robust support for multilingual models, including Devanagari script, which is critical for parsing commodities distributed outside urban centers34. However, robust multi-script Named Entity Recognition (NER) represents a significant research burden. For the SIH prototype scope, the pipeline should explicitly target English and Hindi to guarantee stability, explicitly marking regional scripts as a target for Future Work [RECOMMENDATION].

R. Dataset Gap Analysis
While raw image datasets of retail shelves and general scene text exist, there is a total absence of a specialized LMPC Compliance Annotated Dataset [FACT]. There are no available public datasets mapping images of Indian packaging explicitly to their Legal Metrology rule violations.
To build an effective evaluation pipeline, the engineering team must construct a bespoke validation dataset comprising approximately 500 to 1,000 images of Indian FMCG products. The required annotation specification must strictly include: Image_ID, Spatial_Bounding_Boxes, OCR_Transcriptions, Key_Value_Semantic_Pairs, and explicit LMPC_Violation_Flags mapped directly to the Rule 6 and Rule 7 clauses [ENGINEERING-INFERENCE].
S. Synthetic Data Research
To rapidly bridge the dataset gap during the pre-engineering phase, synthetic data generation provides a highly viable avenue for training the layout and OCR extraction models. Digital packaging label templates can be procedurally generated and populated with randomized data (fictional addresses, randomized MRPs, and varying dates). These labels can be rendered onto 3D primitives (cylinders, boxes) using Python rendering APIs, subsequently applying environmental augmentations such as blur, low-light, and specular glare.
However, synthetic data cannot reliably model the physical degradation of real-world labels—such as tears, smudges, fading, or genuine compliance edge-cases like an updated MRP sticker placed directly over the original printed label. Therefore, while synthetic data suffices for bootstrapping model weights, real-world image testing remains mandatory for the final performance evaluation [HYPOTHESIS].

Z. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Design
Complete automation of legal compliance enforcement is legally fraught and practically impossible given current dimensional sensing limits. The system must be explicitly designed as an assistive analytical tool for the metrology inspector.
Decision States Machine:
PASS: All rules validated with high confidence thresholds; no anomalies detected.
FAIL: Deterministic violation detected (e.g., MRP string is definitively missing, or Expiry Date is in the past).
NEEDS REVIEW: OCR confidence is low, the LLM schema generator flagged a probable error, or spatial compliances (such as Rule 7 font sizes) require human verification.
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: The extraction pipeline indicates missing panels (e.g., the user only scanned the front of a box, but the manufacturer address rule necessitates scanning the back).
The system integrates a multi-view fusion workflow via an active acquisition loop. If the state is INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, the UI actively prompts the user: "Manufacturer address not found. Please rotate the package and scan the back panel." The system then stitches the extracted JSON states across multiple temporal frames to evaluate compliance [RECOMMENDATION].
AA. Explainability
A completely opaque FAIL output is useless to a metrology inspector who must issue a legally binding challan (citation). System explainability must go beyond generic heatmaps.
The traceability chain must explicitly link: Final Decision  Executed Rule Node  Extracted Key-Value Pair  Raw OCR Text String  Original Image Bounding Box.
The UI compliance report must display the original image with an overlaid bounding box exactly highlighting where the offending text was detected, paired with a direct citation of the violated statute (e.g., "Violation of Rule 6(1)(e): MRP not found").
AB. Security / Privacy
While scanning public product labels does not inherently violate Personally Identifiable Information (PII) laws, the system may process proprietary supply-chain metadata or pre-release product designs during manufacturer self-audits.
The production posture requires strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) to separate field inspectors from system administrators. All scans, algorithmic results, and manual overrides must be written to an immutable cryptographic audit log to prevent tampering. For the SIH prototype, basic JWT authentication and secure API endpoints are sufficient to demonstrate security awareness.

AE. Benchmarking Framework
To objectively evaluate the algorithmic performance of the system, metrics must be tracked across the discrete subsystems:
OCR Subsystem: Character Error Rate (CER) and Word Error Rate (WER). The baseline target is a CER < 5% on clear packaging.
Detection Subsystem: Mean Average Precision (mAP@50). The target is > 90% for detecting the Principal Display Panel region.
Extraction Subsystem: Entity-level F1 Score. The target is > 95% for high-priority numeric fields like MRP and Dates.
End-to-End System: Exact Match Ratio for the final compliance state (PASS/FAIL). The target operational profile is an > 85% automation rate, with the remaining 15% routed to NEEDS REVIEW. The false-positive rate for violations must be strictly constrained to < 1%.

AG. Decision Matrices (Consolidated)
Extraction Architecture: A Hybrid OCR + LLM pipeline with Schema Enforcement (Outlines) was chosen over pure Vision-Language Models due to strict determinism and auditability requirements.
OCR Engine: PaddleOCR was chosen over Tesseract due to published benchmark superiority in handling noisy, curved scene text typical of product packaging31.
Rule Engine: Zen Engine was chosen over Drools due to its Rust-based performance envelope and seamless native integration with Python ML microservices51.
Deployment Target (Prototype): Cloud API inference is recommended for the hackathon phase. While on-device edge execution is the ultimate production goal for field inspectors, packaging heavy ML models into constrained edge devices exceeds the time and scope constraints of the SIH prototyping phase.

AI. Recommended Production Architecture

AK. Testing Strategy
Standard unit testing paradigms are insufficient for probabilistic AI pipelines. The testing strategy mandates a multi-tiered approach:
Rule Unit Tests: Deterministic testing of the Zen Engine decision tables isolating the AI variance.
Case 1: Extracted JSON { "net_quantity": "500", "unit": "g" }  Expect PASS.
Case 2: Extracted JSON { "net_quantity": "500", "unit": "gms" }  Expect FAIL (Rule violation: non-standard unit)18.
Pipeline Integration Tests: Passing a known gold-standard image through the OCR  Extraction  Rules pipeline to ensure end-to-end data coherence.
Regression Testing: When legislative rules update (e.g., an amendment altering Unit Sale Price rounding), historical test cases must be re-run against the newly versioned rule set to guarantee backward compatibility for auditing historical inspections.
AL. SIH Demo Strategy
The hackathon demonstration must conclusively prove functional reality, heavily penalizing precomputed or mocked scripts.
The presentation flow should invite judges to provide a packaged commodity (e.g., a standard water bottle or biscuit pack). The team captures the image live, utilizing the UI to visually expose the intermediate computational steps: rendering the PaddleOCR bounding boxes directly over the image, displaying the parsed intermediate JSON output, and illustrating the Zen Engine decision tree execution path. Finally, the team introduces a modified package (e.g., where the MRP is deliberately obscured) to demonstrate the system successfully identifying a Rule 6 violation in real-time and generating the accurate legal citation. A documented offline fallback plan—maintaining a local server with pre-loaded videos and a network-independent LLM (via Ollama)—must be prepared in case of venue network degradation.

AN. Research Gaps
The primary gap identified in current academic literature and available datasets is the complete absence of a unified framework connecting complex visual document understanding directly to structured, versioned legal syllogisms. While mature models exist to extract key-value pairs from standard receipts (SROIE) and administrative forms (FUNSD)70, evaluating dense extracted packaging data against a dynamic, evolving legal framework (such as the LMPC amendments introduced between 2011 and 2023) represents an entirely unexplored intersection of Regulatory Technology (RegTech) and Computer Vision. Bridging this gap through the rigorous integration of deterministic rule engines (Zen Engine) with probabilistic, multimodal extraction pipelines (OCR + LLM) constitutes the core engineering novelty and ultimate value proposition of the proposed solution.
