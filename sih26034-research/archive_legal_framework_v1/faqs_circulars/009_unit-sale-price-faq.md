## Why this source matters
Following the enforcement of G.S.R. 779(E) and G.S.R. 226(E), the Department of Consumer Affairs issued definitive FAQs addressing industry implementation of Unit Sale Price. This document provides the mathematical formulas and exception cases for the compliance rule engine.

## Core Questions & Legal Interpretations

### Q1. How must Unit Sale Price be computed and declared?
- **Formula**:
  $$\text{Unit Sale Price} = \frac{\text{Maximum Retail Price (MRP)}}{\text{Net Quantity declared in specified standard unit}}$$
- **Rounding Requirement**: Must be rounded off to the **nearest two decimal places** in Indian Rupees. Example: An item of 250 g priced at ₹55.00 has USP $= 55 / 250 = ₹0.22\text{ per g}$.

### Q2. Which standard units must be used as denominators?
- Weight $< 1\text{ kg}$: per **gram** (`Rs. X.XX per g`).
- Weight $> 1\text{ kg}$: per **kilogram** (`Rs. X.XX per kg`).
- Volume $< 1\text{ L}$: per **millilitre** (`Rs. X.XX per ml`).
- Volume $> 1\text{ L}$: per **litre** (`Rs. X.XX per L`).
- Length $< 1\text{ m}$: per **centimetre** (`Rs. X.XX per cm`).
- Length $> 1\text{ m}$: per **metre** (`Rs. X.XX per m`).
- Items sold by count/number: per **number** or **unit** (`Rs. X.XX per N` or `Rs. X.XX per unit`).

### Q3. When is Unit Sale Price exempt from declaration?
1. **MRP Equal to USP**: Where the package contains exactly 1 unit, 1 kg, 1 litre, or 1 metre, and the Retail Sale Price equals the Unit Sale Price, declaration of USP is not mandatory.
2. **Wholesale Packages**: Packages not intended for direct retail sale.
3. **Multi-piece Combination Packages**: Packages containing different dissimilar products sold as a single combo pack.
