## Why this source matters
The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 is the parent statute establishing uniform standards for weights, measures, weighing and measuring instruments, and pre-packaged commodities across the Republic of India. Section 18 and Section 36 establish the direct legal requirement for package declarations and the statutory penalties for violations flagged by computer vision audits.

## Key extracted points
- **Legislative Enactment**: Passed on January 13, 2010; entered into full legal force on April 1, 2011, repealing the Standards of Weights and Measures Act, 1976 and the Standards of Weights and Measures (Enforcement) Act, 1985.
- **Section 2(l) — Statutory Definition of 'Pre-packaged Commodity'**: *"a commodity which without the purchaser being present is placed in a package of whatever nature, whether sealed or not, so that the product contained therein has a pre-determined quantity."*
- **Section 18(1) — Mandatory Packaging Compliance**: Expressly prohibits any manufacturing, packing, sale, distribution, delivery, or offering for sale of any pre-packaged commodity unless it bears prescribed standard declarations, quantities, and marks.
- **Section 36(1) & (2) — Penalties for Non-Conformity**:
  - *First Offence*: Fine up to ₹25,000.
  - *Second Offence*: Fine up to ₹50,000.
  - *Subsequent Offences*: Fine not less than ₹50,000 extending to ₹1,00,000, or imprisonment up to 1 year, or both.
- **Section 49 — Corporate Liability**: Mandates companies designate a Director/Manager responsible for Legal Metrology compliance; absent explicit nomination, every person in charge of business operations is held liable.
- **Section 52 — Rule-Making Authority**: Delegates power to Central Government to prescribe detailed packaging rules (Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011).

## Verbatim excerpts
> "Section 18. (1) No person shall manufacture, pack, sell, distribute, deliver, offer, expose or possess for sale any pre-packaged commodity unless such package is in such standard quantities or number and bears thereon such declarations and marks in such manner as may be prescribed."
>
> "Section 36. (1) Whoever manufactures, packs, imports, sells, distributes, delivers or offers for sale, any pre-packaged commodity which does not conform to the declarations on the package as provided in this Act, shall be punished with fine which may extend to twenty-five thousand rupees, for the second offence, with fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees and for the subsequent offence, with fine which shall not be less than fifty thousand rupees but which may extend to one lakh rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year or with both."
