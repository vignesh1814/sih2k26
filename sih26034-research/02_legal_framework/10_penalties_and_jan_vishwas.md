# Module 10: Penalties and the Jan Vishwas Act, 2023
**Decriminalization and Compounding of Offenses**

## The Impact of the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023
The Jan Vishwas Act overhauled the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, to promote the "Ease of Doing Business." Its primary mechanism was decriminalizing minor, technical offenses (like a missing MRP or incorrect font size) and converting them into civil penalties.

## Key Changes for AI Enforcement
1.  **Removal of Imprisonment**: For a first offense under Section 36 (manufacturing/selling non-standard packages) or Section 52 (violating rules), imprisonment is largely removed.
2.  **Compounding**: The offenses detected by the AI (e.g., Rule 6 violations) are now "compoundable." This means the company can pay a fine to settle the matter without going to court.
3.  **Improvement Notices**: The law now emphasizes "Improvement Notices" for first-time, minor labeling errors rather than immediate heavy fines.
4.  **Automatic Escalation**: Penalties are legally mandated to increase by 10% every three years.

## AI Penalty Matrix (Post-Jan Vishwas)
When the AI detects a violation, the generated report should cite the specific section for compounding:
*   **Violation**: Missing MRP, Mfg Date, or Manufacturer Address (Rule 6).
*   **Statute Triggered**: Section 36(1) of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 (Manufacturing/selling non-standard packages).
*   **Penalty Action**: The AI should recommend the issuance of a Compounding Notice. Under the amended Act, the Adjudicating Officer levies a civil penalty. If the AI detects this is a repeat offense (via database lookup of the manufacturer), the fine multiplies.
